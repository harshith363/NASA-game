# Phase 1 science review

- Apollo 11 landed on July 20, 1969. Eagle's lower stage stayed at Tranquility Base while the upper stage departed. The coordinates come from the NASA Lunar Surface Journal and describe the approximate landing site. The LRO photograph is historical evidence, while the diagram illustrates stage separation. [Mission overview](https://www.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/static/history/alsj/a11/a11ov.html), [LRO photograph](https://science.nasa.gov/resource/apollo-11-landing-site/), [mission log](https://www.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/static/history/ap11ann/apollo11_log/log.htm).
- Opportunity landed on January 24, 2004 Pacific time (January 25 UTC). It was designed for 90 sols, drove 45.16 km, made its last contact on June 10, 2018, and its mission was declared complete on February 13, 2019. Perseverance Valley is the approximate final region. [Mission record](https://science.nasa.gov/mission/mer-opportunity/), [mission end](https://science.nasa.gov/missions/mer/nasas-opportunity-rover-mission-on-mars-comes-to-end/), [official map](https://science.nasa.gov/photojournal/opportunitys-final-traverse-map/).
- Layered rocks and mineral measurements are evidence for ancient water-related conditions. Opportunity did not discover life. [El Capitan image and science](https://science.nasa.gov/photojournal/evidence-of-a-water-soaked-past/), [jarosite measurement](https://science.nasa.gov/photojournal/mineral-tells-tale-of-watery-past-2/).
- The final record shows NASA's partial full-frame Pancam transmission. NASA describes it as the final transmitted data product and separately notes that thumbnails from a set acquired about three minutes later were also transmitted. The shadow photograph dates to July 2004 and is a separate historical image. [Pancam context](https://science.nasa.gov/resource/opportunitys-last-message/), [shadow photograph](https://science.nasa.gov/resource/opportunity-catches-its-shadow/).
